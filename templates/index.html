Financial Literacy & Educational Guide

Simplified guidelines to help you make smart borrowing decisions.

1. Understanding Credit Score (CIBIL)

Your CIBIL score is a 3-digit number (300-900) summarizing your credit history. Scores above 750 get faster approvals and lower interest rates. Applying to multiple banks at once creates multiple 'hard inquiries' which lowers your score.

2. What is Debt-To-Income (DTI)?

DTI measures how much of your monthly income goes toward paying debts (including the new EMI). Lenders prefer a DTI below 50%. A high DTI makes banks see you as high-risk.

3. Looking Beyond Interest Rates

Processing fees (which can be flat or percentage-based) and insurance premiums are often added to the loan cost. A loan with 9% rate and high fees might cost more than a 9.5% loan with zero fees.

4. Prepayment Penalties

If you get extra funds and want to close your loan early, some banks charge a prepayment penalty (e.g. 2-4% of outstanding principal). Look for loans with 0% prepayment penalty if you plan early closure.
